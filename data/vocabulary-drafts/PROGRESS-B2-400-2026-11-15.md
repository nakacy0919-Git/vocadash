# B2 expansion — 400 headword drafts, 2026-11-15

## Added

400 previously undrafted B2 headwords, from **bikini** through **telescope** in the remaining master sequence, were authored in two internal batches of 200 records. Every record contains an English definition, Japanese meaning, part of speech, two original English sentences with Japanese translations, a collocation and a Japanese usage note.

There are **800 new bilingual examples** and **274 nonempty contextual synonym/paraphrase candidate fields**. Candidates have restrictions by sense, grammar, register or domain. Explanatory paraphrases are not presented as single-word synonyms. The other entries explicitly retain unassigned-synonym notes.

All entries remain `draft-unreviewed`. Source levels apply to headwords and are not claims about example-sentence difficulty. No images were generated.

## Verification and editorial limits

- Draft validator: zero errors and zero warnings for required fields, counts, English/Japanese text, unique sense/example IDs, exact duplicate English sentences and current export/source correspondence.
- Private master reconciliation: zero word-ID/headword mismatches.
- The hash index covers all 57 batches.
- A conservative check flagged 170 coordinated definitions for manual sense-boundary review. Some are one broad sense; others need separate sense records and additional examples before publication. See `quality/b2-400-semantic-review.json`. These flags are not grammatical validation failures and are not marked resolved.
- The entire dataset still needs human semantic review, naturalness checks and Japanese translation review. The historical 53-word synonym-status backlog is also retained.

Corrections to coarse master labels are kept separate from the preserved source IDs: hone, repaint, roam and sag are authored as verbs; referent is authored as a noun. Plural display forms are supplied for ravages and tatters. The historical council sense of soviet is displayed in lowercase, while the original ID is preserved.

## Terms requiring context

Offensive or outdated labels have original metalinguistic examples and explicit usage notes. Recognition-only recommendations for cripple, dumb, gook and sexy are authoring metadata; they do not implement runtime filtering. Medical terms are used for vocabulary comprehension without diagnostic or treatment instructions. No runtime behavior changed.
